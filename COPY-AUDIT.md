# Wordage pass: research findings and section-by-section audit

Carter's notes from July 8: study real ecommerce sites, work on wordage.
This document is the study pass and the audit. The rewrite in index.html
follows directly from it. Structure, layout, and the three elements he
liked (results cards, flywheel, story animation) are untouched.

## What the study pass found

Sites reviewed: Common Thread Collective, Tinuiti, NoGood, Canopy
Management (agencies), plus the copy playbooks of Graza, Magic Spoon,
and Liquid Death (DTC voice).

Five patterns showed up across every strong agency site:

1. **Operator language, not poetry.** The best agencies talk in the
   vocabulary of a founder's P&L: profit, contribution margin, wasted
   spend, forecast. CTC literally calls itself an "ecommerce profit
   partner" and frames its whole pitch around financial outcomes.
   Tinuiti's entire brand is growth vs waste. The buyer is a founder
   who lives in a spreadsheet, and the copy meets them there.

2. **Proof numbers appear immediately.** Canopy opens with managed
   revenue and brand count. CTC leads with cumulative profitable
   growth engineered and shows aggregate client metrics (YoY revenue,
   contribution margin). NoGood cites its client retention rate.
   Nobody makes the visitor scroll to the case studies section to see
   the first number. Our page currently holds all proof until section 7.

3. **The problem framing is structural, not emotional.** CTC: brands
   grow revenue but cannot explain where the profit went; growth split
   across three roles loses clarity every handoff. Canopy: you end up
   managing four agency relationships that do not talk to each other.
   These are the same ideas as our story section, told in business
   terms instead of literary ones. That is exactly the adjustment
   Carter asked for.

4. **Concrete named deliverables.** Strong sites name the actual work:
   Sponsored Products, DSP, listing optimization, creative testing,
   incrementality. Vague verbs (elevate, unlock, empower) are the tell
   of a weak site.

5. **DTC brand voice (Graza, Magic Spoon): clarity first, personality
   second.** Graza's whole playbook is removing intimidation and
   sounding like a friend, but every playful line still carries a
   concrete fact. Personality survives the rewrite; vagueness does not.

## Section-by-section: before, after, why

### 01 Hero
- Before: "You made something people love. / Selling it became the
  second job."
- After: "You build the product. / We build the demand."
- Why: the old headline was all problem, no promise. The new one is the
  promise in eight words, in the founder's own grammar. It was already
  the best line on the page (buried at the end of chapter 4). Promoted.
- Sub now names the four channels, the deliverable categories, and two
  real proof numbers (Bucket Golf, Corks Popcorn) so the first screen
  passes the proof-immediately test. "Second job" survives here, where
  it lands as a benefit instead of a mood.
- Secondary CTA now points at results ("See the numbers") because proof
  is what founders click. Primary CTA names the real offer: a free
  consultation.

### 02 Marquee: unchanged (channel names, already concrete).

### 03 Story (animation KEPT, chapter copy reworded)
- Head: "If this is you, keep reading" became "It usually starts like
  this." Same invitation, less coy.
- Chapter 2 now names what each platform actually demands (indexed
  listings, weekly creative, creators over ads, Walmart's spec sheets)
  instead of gesturing at "algorithms."
- Chapter 3 title "The math stopped working" was the most literary line
  on the page and Carter quoted it. Now "Every channel became a job,"
  with a body about bids, creative, inventory, and support tickets:
  the actual Tuesday of the founder reading it.
- Chapter 4 no longer ends on the line that moved to the hero.

### 04 Turn: kept. It is Carter's own line and the hinge of the page.
  Sub unchanged.

### 05 Services (flywheel KEPT)
- "Every lane. One team." kept: it was already concrete.
- Lede reframed from "stitching five vendors" prose into the structural
  version the research favors: multiple retainers, multiple contacts,
  nobody owning the final number.
- Cards: each now names deliverables (Brand Stores, keyword research,
  affiliate programs). Removed the one joke that undercut the claim
  ("though they will also look pretty").

### 06 Channels
- "Four channels deep. Not forty channels thin." was the other line
  Carter quoted. Replaced with the most concrete headline possible:
  "Amazon, Meta, TikTok, Walmart. Run properly." The depth argument
  moved into the "And beyond" panel where it does real work.
- TikTok panel now names Shop listings, affiliate programs, and Spark
  Ads. Walmart panel names item setup and Walmart Connect. Walmart tag
  "The next land grab" became "Early advantage."

### 07 Results: KEPT. "Results you can find in a P&L" is exactly the
  operator language the research validates (CTC frames everything in
  P&L terms). Cards untouched.

### 08 Principles
- "We'd rather be useful than comfortable" became "What it's like to
  work with us": a plain promise instead of a clever one. The three
  principles stay; bodies lightly tightened.

### 09 Contact
- "You've got something worth scaling" became "Ready to get your week
  back?": closes the loop with the hero's second-job idea, as a benefit.
- Sub replaced humble-agency talk with the offer: describe where you
  are stuck, get a straight answer about whether we can move it.
- Submit button: "Get a straight answer," which is the principles
  section made into a CTA.

### 10 Footer: unchanged (tagline is the official brand line).

## Rules honored
No em or en dashes anywhere. Only verified case-study numbers. Narrative
order intact: problem, turn, solution, evidence, mirrored close. Results
cards, flywheel, and story animation untouched at the structural level.
